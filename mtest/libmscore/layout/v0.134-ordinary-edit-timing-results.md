dominate the comparison. No corresponding Studio timer is available.

Both experimental blocks contain five plan calls per command and no merged
geometry or grace-distance stage. The slur block typically validates five
chord contexts and extracts ten element shapes: consistent with the audited
early ordinary-slur rejection. The hairpin block typically validates 112
contexts and extracts 248 element shapes, consistent with full ordinary plan
processing. Existing timers count attempts, not explicit acceptance results;
do not describe them alone as proof of every final plan's acceptance.

These controls are much faster than the earlier merged-grace score's 51.469 ms
median. They show that the major slowdown is not reproduced by either small
ordinary fixture. They do not prove that all ordinary scores are fast, or that
moving an ordinary item in a system containing grace groups avoids their work.

## Neighboring beam change remains unresolved

The two screenshots visibly show a different beam placement/slant in N2 after
editing N1's slur. The user reports a similar change while editing a hairpin.
Record this as a repeat-layout inconsistency, not an established fallback.

The slur fixture already has an unsupported ordinary main-chord slur before
editing. All measured experimental commands show the same early-rejection
pattern. That does not support a new spacing fallback caused by the grip edit.

Beam::layout2 selects its modern solver from beamLayout4, manual beam status,
tablature and supported tremolo context. It does not consult spacingLayout4.
The two fixtures have ordinary pitched, unmodified beams and no tremolos.
Slur or hairpin offsets are not direct beam admission conditions. The modern
anchor solver accepts a nonempty pitched group with chord endpoints. However,
the current logs contain neither per-beam branch results nor geometry, so the
specific cause of the observed N2 change is not established by this audit.

Do not disable or broaden fallback gates, change nominal stem lengths, or
claim a beam fix based only on these screenshots. A useful regression should
compare N2's stem lengths and primary beam before/after N1 grip commits and
across repeated full and range layouts, with each experimental style retained.
